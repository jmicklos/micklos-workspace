## 2026-06-12 — Roof rack removed, then water leak discovered

### Situation
- Jonathan removed the old roof rack and the four hardened-plastic mount spacers that were epoxied into the roof's drip/water-runoff channels. The spacers sat on top of the factory holes for factory hardware.
- After pulling the grommets, he ran the 4Runner through a car wash and got water in the cabin at **all four corners**.

### Root cause (confirmed)
- The open factory bolt holes in the drip channels are the leak source. The rack mounts/grommets were the only water seal. A prior car wash *with the rack on* was fine; the first wash *after removal* flooded the interior — confirming the holes are the cause, not a coincidental leak.

### What Jonathan Said
- "I removed the roof rack but now I have four remaining mounts that I had epoxied in place that I need to remove"
- Mounts are "some kind of hardened plastic... black... bonded to the painted roof inside of the channel for the water runoff... basically spacers on top of the holes that already exist from the factory."
- "I went to a car wash and now I have water in my car and all corners. what the fuck?"
- "I had a recent car wash when the rack was on so this would definitely be the root cause."

### Guidance given
- **Mount removal:** pry/shear cold first (brittle plastic), mild heat (~150–200°F) only if needed to soften epoxy, plastic razor for residue, Goo Gone/3M for film. Avoid acetone on paint.
- **Drying (urgent):** towels/shop vac, lift carpet edges, DampRid, heat + fan — standing water → mold/rust/wiring corrosion within days.
- **Temp seal:** butyl tape over open holes to prevent re-flooding before parts arrive.
- **Permanent seal:** touch up bare metal in channel, then OEM body plugs (or correctly-sized rubber plugs) seated with butyl/seam sealer — channel holds water by design, so plug must be sealed not just press-fit. Alternatively bolts + rubber washers + RTV, or just install the new rack (its mounts re-seal the holes).
