# Home – Remodel – Living Room TV

## Goal
Mount a TV above the living room fireplace with clean audio integration.

## Plan

### Audio decision — DECIDED (REVERSED): Sonos Beam + existing Sub (2026-06-23)
Using the **Sonos Beam and Sub Jonathan already owns** — no new purchase. 3.1 system on the mantel. Accepting one extra HDMI cable down the left surround as the tradeoff for free, better-sounding, ecosystem-native audio. This reverts the brief Samsung detour and returns to Jonathan's original "stay in Sonos" instinct.

**Chosen: Sonos Beam (owned) + Sonos Sub (owned) — 3.1**
- Beam is **Gen 1 = ~4" deep** (NOT the ~2.7" Gen 2). Mantel is only 2.33" deep → Beam overhangs by ~1.6". **Does not fit the mantel as-is.**
- **Leading fix: have Tim deepen the mantel shelf to ~5"** (4" bar + breathing room) so the Beam sits fully supported. Low-friction since the remodel is active and Tim is engaged; merges with the still-open wall-blocking task. Question for Tim: can he deepen just the mantel without throwing off the fireplace/surround proportions?
- Fallback A: Sonos official Beam wall-mount bracket below the surround (depth becomes a non-issue, but a visible black bar under the Frame TV + needs blocking at that spot).
- Fallback B: upgrade to Gen 2 Beam (~2.7", fits with slim overhang) — but ~$500 new spend, undercuts the "use what I own" rationale. Last resort.
- Existing Sub covers the Beam's only weakness (bass) — beats the slim S801D where it's weakest
- $0 spent (Beam + Sub owned); stays in the Sonos ecosystem (multiroom, one app)
- **Cable cost:** Beam connects via HDMI eARC to the One Connect box (floor, left). So TWO cables run down the left surround — One Invisible Connection + slim white HDMI — bundled and white, reads as ~one line. Jonathan accepted this ("saving the money and sacrificing with an ugly cable").

**Why not wireless:** Sonos fundamentally cannot connect a soundbar to the TV wirelessly, and cannot use wireless speakers as FRONT channels (wireless satellites can only be surrounds). Samsung Q-Symphony is the only wireless-to-TV path — that was the entire (and only) reason the S801D was ever considered.

<details>
<summary>Rejected — Samsung S801D wireless route ($800)</summary>

- Samsung S801D (~1.6" deep, fits mantel), wireless Q-Symphony to Frame TV → only ONE cable along surround
- Pro: no cable to the soundbar (the only thing Sonos can't do)
- Con: $800 new spend, worse sound than Beam+Sub (small drivers, slim sub, no standalone sub upgrade path), leaves Sonos ecosystem
- Rejected 2026-06-23: the single saved cable runs down the same left edge anyway, bundled with the One Invisible Connection — not worth $800 + a sound downgrade
</details>

<details>
<summary>Considered & rejected — Sonos 4.1/5.1 with surrounds or an Amp</summary>

- Amp + passive front L/R = true 4.1, but forces visible speakers flanking the floor-level fireplace (no furniture, no Smurf tube) — abandons the Beam and the clean-look constraint
- Beam + Amp/Era surrounds = 5.1, but adds visible wall speakers (in-wall/ceiling already rejected as too much construction)
- Decision: ship Beam + Sub (3.1) now; treat "add surrounds" as a separate future project (wireless Era 100s if ever wanted)
</details>

- **Source hub:** Samsung One Connect box (13.7×2.6×5.4") **placed IN the alcove** — power + Ethernet are already there. (REVISED 2026-06-24, was "floor level, left side." Pending physical fit check.) All HDMI sources plug in here.
- **Audio:** Sonos Beam (Gen 1, ~4" deep) + Sub (both owned) on the mantel — 3.1. Beam connects via HDMI eARC to the One Connect box. **Mantel must be deepened to ~5" to seat the Beam — Tim confirmed feasible 2026-06-24.**
- **Cable routing:** With the box in the alcove, the **base setup needs no visible surround cables** — the One Invisible Connection (TV→box) stays in the alcove, and the Beam→box HDMI eARC is a short hop from the mantel up into the alcove. Cables down the left surround only if accessories are added later. (REVISED 2026-06-24, was "two cables down the left.")
- **Mount:** Tim confirmed the mount approach makes sense (2026-06-23)
