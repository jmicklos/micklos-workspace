## Thermal Envelope

### Stairwell & Stack Effect
- **Height differential:** ~25–30 ft from basement windows to stairwell skylight
- Two stairwells stacked vertically; top stairwell (Main ↔ Top) always open
- Bottom stairwell (Main ↔ ADU) separated by boundary door
- Door closed = two thermal zones; door open = one unified zone
- Stair treads are solid (limits convection even when door open)
- Ground-cooled basement air: ~10–13°C year-round from soil mass

### Tree Canopy (all deciduous — ideal passive solar)
- **West/NW — Common Hornbeam row** (Carpinus betulus 'Fastigiata', 25" diameter, ~40–50 ft tall): Shades entire west approach in summer; drops leaves in winter for solar gain. SDOT owned (TRE-46779). Root damage to sidewalk (slab jack repair planned).
- **SW — Saucer Magnolia** (Magnolia × soulangeana, pink flowers): Shades SW corner in summer.
- **NE — Neighbor's ash** (unidentified): Shades morning sun on NE corner.

### Thermal Implications
- **Main floor west/SW:** Heavily shaded by Hornbeams + Magnolia in summer — floor-mount units need less cooling than BTU numbers suggest
- **Top floor:** Above canopy influence — FDMQ18 fights unshaded solar load + skylights compound heat gain/loss
- **NVM inlet air:** Hornbeam microclimate cools west-facing yard air below ambient readings
