## ★★★★★ (5) — landed hardest

### The Color of Law — Richard Rothstein (5★, read 2018-07)
**Your take (quoted):** *"This will likely be my go-to book for book recommendations… 71 pages of notes and bibliography… The completeness of Rothstein's writing has substantially impacted my understanding of US history — at times reinforcing and at times upending. I wish I had had this book during my own high school / college education."*
**Thesis:** US residential segregation was not incidental "de facto" sorting but *de jure* — created and enforced by government at every level (federal redlining, FHA loan denial, public-housing siting, zoning, court-enforced covenants). Because it was state action, it is a constitutional violation demanding remedy, not just regret.
**Ideas worth carrying:**
- The de facto / de jure distinction is load-bearing — "it just happened that way" is often a cover story for policy that was deliberate and documented.
- Systemic outcomes compound across generations (denied home equity → denied inherited wealth). Present gaps have traceable historical mechanisms.
- Evidentiary density *is* the argument: 71 pages of notes is why it persuaded him.
**Decision-relevance to you:** This is the clearest proof of what earns your trust — completeness, receipts, and an author willing to upend your priors with evidence. When Claude argues anything structural to you, it should bring the mechanism and the sources, not the conclusion.

## ★★ (2) — disliked

### Fight Oligarchy — Bernie Sanders (2★, read 2026-07)
**Your take (quoted):** *"Very high level and low on substance… as an engineer, having read this book leaves me upset that you present your life's work as not well thought through… it fails to provide explanations or mechanisms on the sustainability of those recommendations, leaving the reader concerned for how well thought out those positions are… It felt like I was reading an extended collage of brief campaign clips."*
**Thesis (book's):** A populist case against concentrated wealth/power and a call to organize for workers' rights and economic democracy.
**Why it fell flat for you:** Not the values — the rigor. You separate *agreeing with a conclusion* from *accepting the argument*. Vision without mechanism reads as unserious to you, and you'd rather a cause you support be argued well.
**Decision-relevance to you:** The single most useful data point for how to advise you. If a recommendation is long on aspiration and short on "here's the mechanism and why it sustains," you will distrust it — **even when you share the goal.** Lead with mechanism.
