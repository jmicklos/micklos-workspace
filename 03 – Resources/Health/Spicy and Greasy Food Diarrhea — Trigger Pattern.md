# Spicy & Greasy Food Diarrhea — Trigger Pattern

*Reference note to take into a GI appointment. Captures the pattern, the mechanism, and the specific things to ask a doctor.*

## The pattern (my actual history)

- I eat **very spicy food frequently** → **frequently get diarrhea** as a result.
- When I **stop eating spicy food**, the diarrhea **stops completely**.
- I **avoid greasy breakfasts** for the same reason — greasy food reliably does the same thing to me.
- So I have **two independent food triggers** (capsaicin + fat) that produce the **same reaction**, and the reaction **cleanly reverses** when I remove the triggers.

This is, in effect, a clean **dechallenge/rechallenge**: symptom appears with exposure, disappears without it. Strong evidence the trigger is real, not coincidence.

### Trigger table (every data point lines up on FAT)

| Food | Fat? | Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| Raw spicy pepper | No | ✅ Fine |
| Greasy breakfast | Yes (no capsaicin) | ❌ Trigger |
| Nuggets + mayo + reaper sauce | Yes | ❌ Trigger |
| Mala ramen (chili oil + fatty broth) | Yes | ❌ Trigger |

Mala is the purest case: 麻辣 is chili + Sichuan peppercorn **suspended in chili oil**, plus a fatty broth — a bowl of both triggers at once. **Signal is clean: fat triggers me (with or without capsaicin); fat + capsaicin is worst-case; heat alone never does.**

## KEY FINDING (2026-08-10): it's fat, not heat

Ran the clean-heat test accidentally and got a clear answer:

- **A very spicy pepper eaten alone → FINE.**
- **Chicken nuggets (fried) + mayo + reaper sauce → TRIGGERS it.**

The nugget meal is capsaicin delivered *in fat* (fried nuggets + mayo). The pepper is capsaicin with ~no fat. **Capsaicin alone is fine; capsaicin delivered in fat is the problem.** Two mechanisms, same direction:

1. **Fat is a trigger on its own** — consistent with the greasy-breakfast trigger. Fat drives the gastrocolic reflex and is the classic BAM trigger.
2. **Fat weaponizes the capsaicin.** Capsaicin is fat-soluble. A dry pepper keeps it locked in fiber; mayo + fried fat **dissolves it into an emulsion** and carries it, bioavailable, along the whole gut wall. The mayo+reaper sauce was effectively a **capsaicin tincture**.

**The lever is not "less heat" — it's "heat without fat."**

- **Predicted SAFE:** fresh/raw peppers, dry chili flakes, dry rubs, **vinegar-based hot sauces** (Tabasco / Louisiana-style, near-zero fat), kimchi.
- **Predicted TRIGGER:** chili suspended in fat — mayo/butter/cream sauces, **chili crisp / chili oil** (literally capsaicin in oil — expected trigger despite being "milder"), fried food carrying heat.
- **Testable prediction:** a punishing amount of vinegar hot sauce on non-greasy food = fine; modest chili crisp = trouble.

This **strengthens the BAM angle** — fat is the central actor across *both* triggers (breakfast and the sauce).

## The mechanism (why both triggers converge)

Capsaicin and fat look unrelated but hit diarrhea through the **same final pathway — the gastrocolic reflex** (the post-meal signal telling the colon to contract and move things along):

- **Fat** is one of the strongest natural triggers of the gastrocolic reflex (via CCK and related signals). Classic cause of post-meal urgency/diarrhea.
- **Capsaicin** drives motility via **TRPV1 receptors**, which line the *entire* GI tract — including the rectum/anus (that's the burn on the way out). Accelerated transit → colon has less time to reabsorb water → watery stool.

**Key insight:** it's not that capsaicin is *uniquely* my enemy. My gut **over-responds to motility stimulants in general**, and both chili and fat are potent ones. Two different triggers, one shared downstream mechanism.

**Second key insight:** gut TRPV1 desensitizes **far less completely** than the mouth does. So "I eat extreme heat frequently and love it" and "my gut still revolts every time" are fully compatible — different tissues, different adaptation rates. **Building tolerance is NOT my escape hatch** — I'm already a frequent eater and still react every time.

## What it points to

Convergent evidence (two mechanistically-distinct triggers, same reaction, clean reversal) leans toward:

1. **IBS-D (diarrhea-predominant IBS)** — hypersensitive/hyperreactive gut with an exaggerated gastrocolic reflex. Leading hypothesis. TRPV1-hypersensitive IBS is a documented phenotype.
2. **Bile acid malabsorption (BAM)** — commonly missed, very treatable. Bile acids spilling into the colon cause watery, urgent diarrhea **classically triggered by fatty meals**. Treatment = bile acid sequestrant (e.g. cholestyramine). Worth explicitly naming because greasy food is a dependable trigger for me.

**Reassuring:** because it *fully reverses* when I stop, this looks functional/irritant — not an ongoing inflammatory disease (IBD wouldn't switch off cleanly).

## Self-observation to do before the appointment

- **Greasy-meal stool character:** is it *greasy, pale, floating, hard-to-flush*? → points to **fat malabsorption / steatorrhea** (pancreatic, more specific). Plain **watery** → more the IBS/BAM picture.
- **Clean-heat test:** ✅ DONE (accidentally) — raw pepper alone = fine; capsaicin-in-fat (nuggets + mayo + reaper) = trigger. **Result: it's fat, not heat.** See KEY FINDING above.
- **Confirm the fat-carrier theory:** eat a lot of **vinegar hot sauce on non-greasy food** (should be fine) vs. modest **chili crisp / chili oil** (expected trigger). If chili crisp triggers, the fat-carrier mechanism is confirmed.
- **Threshold mapping:** find the heat level below which my gut is fine and above which it isn't. Frequent *moderate* heat may be sustainable where frequent *extreme* heat isn't.

## What to say to the doctor

> "Both spicy food and greasy food reliably give me diarrhea; it stops completely when I avoid them. Notably, a raw spicy pepper alone is fine — but capsaicin mixed with fat (e.g. a mayo-based hot sauce on fried food) reliably triggers it. **Fat seems to be the common thread.**"

Ask specifically about **IBS-D** and **bile acid malabsorption** (the fat-specificity points here).

## Day-to-day management (helps, but isn't the whole answer)

- Never eat spicy on an **empty stomach** (capsaicin is fat-soluble; food buffers it).
- Pair heat with **dairy fat + casein** (yogurt, ghee, coconut milk, lassi — casein strips capsaicin off receptors) and **starch** (rice, bread).
- Cut the **compounding triggers** from spicy meals: **alcohol, caffeine, heavy grease, high-FODMAP garlic/onion**.
- Add **soluble fiber** for bulk.
- For a known blowout event: **loperamide (Imodium)** ~1hr before — special-occasion tool, not daily.

## Bottom line

Two reliable food triggers through a shared mechanism, cleanly reversible. Past the point where yogurt-and-timing is the whole answer — worth a real GI conversation. Leading candidates (IBS-D, BAM) are both **manageable**, and BAM specifically is **treatable**. Not scary.
