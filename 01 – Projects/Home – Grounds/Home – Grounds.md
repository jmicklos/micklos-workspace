# Home – Grounds

**Merged 2026-08-09** from three previously-separate projects — `Backyard Cleanup`, `V1 Landscaping`, and `Front Wrought Iron Fence` — which were really one initiative described as a wishlist. This note is the single strategy + sequence + budget for the whole grounds effort at [[1120 19th Ave E]]. Detailed reference from all three is preserved below.

## Vision
Take the grounds from construction-wreck to **established Tudor woodland street presence** — English estate woodland edge flowing into an architectural entry, front and rear. Do it in the right order so **no work gets torn out later** (the recurring theme across all three source projects).

## Definition of Done (whole initiative)
- **Rear:** cleared of invasives + construction debris, then planted with permanent planters. Fig tree + rose bushes preserved.
- **Front:** sidewalk repaired, parking strip + front beds planted (Tudor woodland palette), entry markers set.
- **Fence:** wrought iron + brick-pier fence designed, permitted, and installed along the front property line, matching the Tudor aesthetic and passing Seattle front-yard fence code.

## Master Sequence & Rationale
```
PHASE 1  Backyard Cleanup (rear prep) ──────────► gates rear planting
   NOW (overdue)
                                    │
PHASE 2  Front: sidewalk repair + parking strip / bed planting
   Sep–Oct 2026 (ideal PNW planting window)
      └─ sets terracotta urn placeholders ON the future fence line
                                    │
PHASE 3  Rear planting (permanent planters)
   Fall 2026, after Phase 1
                                    │
PHASE 4  Front Wrought Iron Fence (piers the urns staged for)
   Design 2026 → build into 2027
```
**Why this order:**
- Cleanup first — can't plant the rear over debris + invasive roots.
- Front planting is deliberately staged so the **urn placeholders mark the future fence line** — the fence (Phase 4) drops in where the urns are, so nothing planted in Phase 2 gets destroyed.
- Fence is last and slowest: it's a permitted masonry build with a real skill gap (see Phase 4). Design can proceed in parallel, but footings wait.
- **Cross-cutting gate — irrigation:** decide *whether irrigation is needed at all* BEFORE Phase 2 planting, because the plant palette determines the answer. If low-water/native (favored), skip it. This also affects Phase 4 (sleeve under the fence line before pouring piers only if irrigation will ever cross it).

## PHASE 4 — Front Wrought Iron Fence · Design 2026 → build into 2027 (was due 2027-07-16)
Brick piers (columns) every 6–10 ft with wrought iron panels between them, along the front property line — matching the Tudor Revival exterior. **This is the fence the urns in Phase 2 stage for.**

### Code constraints (RESOLVED 2026-07-16 — these are the design drivers)
- **FRONT setback fence max = 4 ft** (SMC 23.44.014). Binding constraint on the whole design.
- **Brick piers get NO height exemption** — piers ≤4 ft too. Height measured **including post caps/finials** → build pier body ~42–44" so cap brings total to ≤4 ft. Don't build to 4 ft then add a cap.
- **No permit required** if total ≤4 ft in front AND no masonry element >6 ft. (Masonry >6 ft anywhere triggers a construction permit.)
- **Sight triangles** (SMC 23.54.030) — front corner pier near the driveway can't obstruct vision clearance; may need to drop height there. (Residential exception for <3 parking spaces — confirm w/ SDCI.)
- **Right-of-way** — fence sits in the front setback up to the property line, NOT in public ROW (parking strip/sidewalk zone is likely city ROW). Confirm exact property line vs. ROW before siting piers.
- **Pier lights: AMBIGUOUS** — not addressed in code. If wanted, get a **written SDCI interpretation** (206-684-8600 / seattle.gov/sdci) before building; a verbal OK won't protect against a later complaint.
- **Historic: CLEAR** — not in any historic district (Harvard-Belmont is ~1 mi west). Self-verify building isn't an individual City Landmark: https://web.seattle.gov/dpd/historicalsite/
- **Placement:** fences have NO setback — can sit on side/rear lines; no code limit on pier size/spacing below 6 ft.

**Going taller than 4 ft (if ever wanted):** only options are set back 15 ft (NR front setback → 6 ft legal by right behind that line), grade + retaining wall (combined up to 9'6", fits Jonathan's skillset), or a living hedge (unregulated — pairs well with a 4 ft iron+brick fence). **Variance is realistically a no** (needs physical hardship, not preference). **Decision: 4 ft at the sidewalk is the answer** for street-edge presence — very Tudor-appropriate.

### Neighbor-line issue — THE one that bites (action required)
- **Footing must stay entirely on his side of the line.** A pier footing is wider than the pier; if the pier face is on the line, the footing encroaches (civil trespass). Fix: **inset the pier** so the footing stays on-parcel, OR pour an **eccentric/one-sided footing**, OR get written neighbor agreement.
- **Don't center a pier on the shared line without written consent.** WA good-neighbor law (RCW 16.60) doesn't force a neighbor to consent/pay. Build wholly on his side, footing included.
- Survey shows an existing fence **"ON-LINE"** → possible existing understanding. Have the conversation before pouring.

### The DIY question — can Jonathan do this himself?
**Verdict: yes technically, but the brick piers are the real skill gap.** Jonathan built retaining walls (high-school company) — strong on excavation, layout, base prep, load paths, heavy material. **Gap: never worked with mortar/masonry.** Freestanding brick piers are harder than a wall (must be plumb on all four faces over full height, no reference; mortar feel takes reps; iron panels embed into piers so spacing/plumb must be dead-on).
- **Path 1 — Full DIY:** doable if willing to build a throwaway practice pier and accept a learning curve on a highly visible result.
- **Path 2 — Hybrid (recommended):** DIY excavation/footings/layout (his strengths) → hire a mason for the piers → DIY the iron-panel install. Best quality-for-effort; piers are the one thing you can't easily redo.
- **Path 3 — CMU-core cheat:** stack hollow CMU, rebar + grout-fill the cores, brick-veneer the outside. Block does the structural work and keeps it plumb; brick is the pretty face. More forgiving than solid structural brick.

## Cross-cutting — Irrigation decision (OPEN; reopened 2026-07-18)
**Do we even need irrigation?** Leaning **no**, if the palette is right — resolve BEFORE Phase 2 planting and BEFORE Phase 4 footings.
- **Low-water case (likely viable):** the planned palette (pachysandra, sword/deer fern, native woodland) is low-water once established. PNW winters do most watering; typically only hand-watering the first summer, then near-zero. Small lot + shade from Hornbeams/Magnolia reduces demand further.
- **If a system is eventually wanted:** drip / 1–2 zones can run off the side hose bib + vacuum breaker (likely no permit). Pop-up spray needs a main-line tap → backflow assembly (annual SPU test) + SDCI plumbing permit (bigger commitment).
- **Decision hinge:** finalize plant palette → if all low-water/native, skip irrigation (hand-water year 1). Only revisit if the design includes thirsty plants or a real lawn.
