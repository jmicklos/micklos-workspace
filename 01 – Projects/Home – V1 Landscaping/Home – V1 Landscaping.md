#### DIY Sidewalk Plan (replaces contractor quote)
Estimated total cost: $100-175 vs. $5,000 quoted

**Equipment:** pry bar or digging bar (24"+), sledgehammer, flat shovel, work gloves, safety glasses, closed-toe boots, contractor trash bags
**Materials:** 2x Great Stuff Big Gap Filler (red can, ~$8 each), wood shims/scrap lumber for propping

##### Priority 1 — Remove asphalt patch + parking strip slab (DO FIRST)
These are the immediate tasks. One dump run for both.

**Step 1 — Remove asphalt patch (15-20 min)**
- Thin (1-2") cold-patch layer over concrete — doesn't bond well, comes up easy
- Flat shovel under the edge and stomp/pry — no sledgehammer needed
- Peels up in sheets/chunks. Scrape residue with the flat bar.
- Bag chunks in contractor bags

**Step 2 — Remove parking strip slab with sledgehammer (~2.5' x 4')**
- Wear safety glasses — concrete chips fly
- Start at edges and corners, not the center. The corner near tree roots is already undermined — start there
- Hit at ~45° angle into edges to shear chunks off. Straight-down blows on center just bounce
- Work cracks as they form — hit along the crack to propagate, splitting into liftable pieces
- Target piece size: ~12"x12" (~20 lbs each). Don't pulverize, just break into manageable chunks
- Pry bar to flip/lever chunks out once cracked free
- This slab is likely thinner than main sidewalk (2-3") and already cracked by roots — should break up easier than expected
- Time: 30-60 min if it cooperates, up to 90 min if stubbornly intact
- **Bail-out:** If barely making progress after 20 min, rent a demo hammer from Home Depot (~$75/day) instead
- Bag debris, combine with asphalt for one dump run ($30-40 at Seattle transfer station)

##### Priority 2 — Lift and backfill sunken slab (AFTER demo is done)

**Step 3 — Lever up sunken slab corner**
- Pry bar + fulcrum block (brick or 4x4) at the low corner
- Lift slowly and evenly to avoid cracking — don't pry from just one point
- Over-lift to 4-6" above target — enough to get hands/tools underneath comfortably
- Prop with wood blocks at this height

**Step 4 — Backfill with expanding foam**
- Spray Great Stuff Big Gap Filler (red can) into the void from the exposed edges
- Foam fills around irregular surfaces and tree roots — no tamping needed
- Slab is propped at fixed height so over-expansion just squeezes out the sides
- Let cure per can instructions
- Remove props, lower slab onto foam, check with level against adjacent slabs
- Walk-on ready once cured
- Alternative: crusher run (3/4" minus) if you want adjustability over permanence — tamp with a 2x4, lower slab onto fill, re-lift and adjust if needed

### 🪵 STEP 1 — Site Preparation (do first — most important)

## Scope

- Remove weeds/grass from parking strip and front woodland areas
- Remove invasive roots
- Light grading if needed
- Loosen top 4–6" soil

## Soil improvement

- Add 2–3" compost
- Till or mix into soil

## Woodland base

- Spread 2–3" arborist wood chips across front yard beds
- Keep 3–4" away from tree trunks

**Result:** woodland soil ecosystem + weed suppression.

1. Lay plants on grid first
2. Dig hole slightly larger than plug
3. Plant level with soil
4. Water deeply
