### Outputs & State — Research findings (both checks RESOLVED)
Ran two parallel research agents (web). Findings written into main note under "Findings" sections.

**Height limit (the design driver):**
- Seattle **FRONT setback fence max = 4 ft** (SMC 23.44.014).
- **Brick piers are NOT height-exempt** — held to the same 4 ft cap in front. Piers ≤4 ft.
- **No permit** if ≤4 ft front and no masonry over 6 ft.
- Sight triangles (SMC 23.54.030) at driveway/corner; don't build in public ROW (parking strip is likely city ROW — confirm property line).
- Side/rear allow 6 ft solid / 8 ft total — only front is capped at 4 ft.
- Verify directly before build: NR sub-zone, ECA/flood overlay, exact ROW line. SDCI (206) 684-8600.

**Historic (clear):**
- **NOT in any historic district.** Nearest (Harvard-Belmont) is ~1 mi west, bounded ~10th Ave E; parcel is at 19th. Capitol Hill Arts District has no design authority. No citywide review for a fence on a 1931 home unless individually landmarked (unlikely).
- Self-verify individual landmark status: https://web.seattle.gov/dpd/historicalsite/
- District map: https://data-seattlecitygis.opendata.arcgis.com/maps/historic-and-special-review-districts

### Design implication (important for next session)
The 4 ft cap INCLUDING piers reframes the project: this is a **low wrought-iron-and-brick garden wall**, ~4 ft, not a tall imposing fence. Classic Tudor look. If Jonathan wanted something taller up front, code says no. Design work should assume 4 ft.

## 2026-07-16 — Finials & lights vs. the 4 ft cap

### What Jonathan Said
- Asked whether the 4 ft cap includes light fixtures and finials. (Thinking about pier ornamentation/lighting — relevant to Tudor aesthetic.)

### Findings (research agent, web)
- **Finials / decorative pier caps: COUNTED.** SMC 23.44.014 measures height "including post caps." The ≤10" architectural-feature exception applies to the 8 ft side/rear context, not the 4 ft front setback — so no extra headroom. Design rule captured in note: build pier body ~42–44" so cap/finial totals ≤4 ft.
- **Light fixtures: AMBIGUOUS.** Code silent; no exempt-lamp-post rule. SDCI interpretation call. If lights wanted, get a WRITTEN SDCI interpretation before building (206-684-8600). Added as a task.

### Design implication
Ornamentation eats into the 4 ft budget. If Jonathan wants a nice finial/cap look, the brick pier itself must be shorter (~42–44"). Lighting on piers is the one open code risk — resolve with SDCI in writing if he wants it, before design is finalized.

## 2026-07-16 — "This is absurdly restrictive" — paths to a taller fence

### What Jonathan Said
- Frustrated by the 4 ft cap: "This is absurdly restrictive. if I want a proper fence then what do I do? can I get a permit?"
- He wants a PROPER (~6 ft) street fence. Reading: he cares about real street presence, not just a low garden wall. Goal not yet pinned (presence vs. privacy vs. max height) — asked him to clarify.

### Findings (research agent, web — OPCD One Seattle Plan Ch. 23.44 PDF + SDCI)
- **The 4 ft cap only applies INSIDE the required front setback.** NR front setback = **15 ft** (SMC 23.44.014 Table A; averaging N/A here). **Behind the 15 ft line a 6 ft fence is legal BY RIGHT** (+2 ft architectural features), minor construction permit. This is the realistic path — tradeoff is it's 15 ft into the yard, not at the sidewalk.
- **Variance:** technically possible (Type II MUP, SMC 23.40.020) but bar is undue hardship from unique physical site condition, NOT aesthetics. Preference-driven requests routinely denied. Months + hourly fees + neighbor appeal. Advised against.
- **Grade + retaining wall:** height measured from grade; grade-raising wall + fence allowed to combined **9'6"**. Plays to Jonathan's retaining-wall background. Front may slope (Hornbeam/sidewalk notes). Still behind 15 ft line for the tall portion.
- **Living hedge:** not regulated as a fence — 6 ft+ screening at the sidewalk, no height limit. Pairs with a 4 ft iron/brick fence.

### Design implication / open decision
Told Jonathan the honest tradeoff is PLACEMENT not possibility: 6 ft is available if set back 15 ft; 4 ft is the max at the sidewalk. Asked him to name the actual goal — **street-edge presence / privacy-screening / max-height** — because they lead to different designs. Also flagged the grade+retaining-wall angle as the one that fits his skillset if the front slopes.

### Next Steps
1. Get Jonathan's real goal (presence vs. privacy vs. height) — this is the fork.
2. Verify exact zone (NR1/2/3), dwelling-unit count, and whether the front frontage slopes (determines if the 9'6" grade play is live).

### Findings — Irrigation supply (research agent, web; SPU + SDCI)
- Pop-up spray forces the **main-line tap** (option 2). Hose bib (~10 GPM, 2–4 heads) can't feed pop-up. Tee downstream of meter → 1" line → backflow (PVB/DCVA) → valve manifold.
- Backflow tested at install + **annually, reported to SPU**. **SDCI plumbing permit** required. Measure static psi early (Capitol Hill up-slope, maybe low end 40–80; >80 needs PRV).
- **This is a bigger sub-system than the fence** (permit + annual obligation). Belongs to V1 Landscaping, NOT the fence.
