Dear Dr. Foong,

I live a few blocks from the Asian Art Museum in Capitol Hill and I'm hoping you might be able to help me understand a small collection of Chinese sculptures I own — or point me toward someone who can.

I purchased five carved and painted figures from an antique gallery in Thailand a few years ago. I believe they are Chinese temple or shrine figures, but I've never been able to confirm what they actually are. The group includes:

- A two-figure composition of what appears to be a man seated atop a subdued demon on an integrated base (which I suspect may be Zhong Kui)
- A standing figure holding a rectangular document or register
- A seated figure on a throne base with a tall crown and civil robes
- Two smaller kneeling armored figures (one red-faced, one pale-faced) that appear to be a matched pair

Three of the five appear to be wood. The two smaller warrior figures feel like they may be a different material — possibly plaster or clay, though I'm not sure. Four of the five have rectangular hollows cut into the back (which I've read may be spirit cavities). There may also be painted marks or an inscription on the reverse of one of the warrior figures.

I'm not primarily looking for a valuation — I'm trying to understand what these figures represent, where they likely came from, and what their original context was. My fiancee is Singaporean Chinese and we're trying to figure out together which pieces we want to keep in our home and which we should find a more appropriate home for. Having proper identification would help us make those decisions respectfully.

I have detailed photographs of all five pieces from multiple angles. I'd be happy to share them by email, or since I'm just down the hill, I could bring a piece or two by if that would be helpful.

Thank you for your time — I realize this isn't a standard curatorial inquiry, but any guidance would be greatly appreciated.

Warm regards,
Jonathan Micklos
