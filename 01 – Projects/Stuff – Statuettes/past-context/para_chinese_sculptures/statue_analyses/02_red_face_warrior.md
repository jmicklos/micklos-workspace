# Statue 2 — Red-Face Kneeling Warrior

## Working Identity
**Chinese polychrome wooden figure of a martial deity — likely Guan Yu (关羽) type**  
The deep vermilion face is a near-definitive iconographic marker. Red-faced martial deities in Chinese temple sculpture almost universally refer to Guan Yu or figures within his cult. Guan Yu was among the most widely worshipped deities in Ming-dynasty southern China, venerated in both Buddhist and Daoist temple contexts.

Alternative: one of the Four Heavenly Kings' generals (e.g., Zeng Jia 增加).

## Physical Description
- **Posture:** Kneeling/seated warrior — same format as Statue 1
- **Face:** Deep red (vermilion) — the defining iconographic feature; wide-set eyes, open mouth, fierce/protective expression
- **Headdress:** Tall official's military cap — same type as Statue 1
- **Armor:** Dragon breastplate, armored sleeves, knee guards — same tradition as Statue 1 but rendered with slightly different compositional compression
- **Build:** Slightly stockier and more compact than Statue 1
- **Left hand:** Holds cylindrical object (largely lost/damaged) — likely originally a weapon haft or scepter
- **Base:** Mounted on modern black lacquered display base (not original)

## Key Authenticity Markers
- **Spirit cavity (藏腹):** Present on reverse — somewhat larger and more roughly finished than Statue 1's cavity. Same temple consecration tradition.
- **Painted decorative elements on reverse:** Back of lower body/belt area shows what appear to be painted marks — potentially inscription or decorative motif. **Warrants close examination and possible expert reading.**
- **Paint loss pattern:** More extensive on reverse than Statue 1; red pigment survives more fully on front surfaces

## Condition
- More extensive paint loss on reverse than Statue 1
- One hand largely missing/damaged
- Spirit cavity open, contents long gone
- Front surfaces retain more original color than reverse

## Probable Origin & Date
- **Southern Chinese workshop** — Fujian and Guangdong provincial temple sculpture particularly associated with Guan Yu worship
- **Ming dynasty, approximately 16th–17th century**
- Same workshop tradition as Statue 1; very likely same commission

## Relationship to Collection
Almost certainly a **matched pair** with Statue 1. The two together flanked a central deity figure (Statue 4) as altar guardians. The red face vs. pale face pairing is a common southern Chinese temple convention — one guardian fierce/wrathful, one calm/composed.

**Note for appraiser:** Paired or grouped temple figures are considerably more valuable and historically significant than individual pieces. Keep paired status prominent in any appraisal context.
