# Statue 1 — Pale-Face Kneeling Warrior

## Working Identity
**Chinese polychrome wooden figure of a military deity or divine guardian (武将/武神)**  
Most likely: kneeling military attendant within the Guan Yu retinue, or a subordinate guardian of a Heavenly King. Not a supreme deity — a ranked attendant.

## Physical Description
- **Posture:** Kneeling/seated warrior
- **Face:** Pale/natural skin tone, calm expression, slight downward gaze
- **Headdress:** Tall official's military cap — Ming-period martial deity iconography
- **Armor:** Elaborate lacquered armor with dragon motifs on breastplate (hallmark of high-ranking divine military figures)
- **Gesture:** Left hand holds a tablet or flat object — offering or reporting posture
- **Lower body:** Armored skirt with fringed tassets, knee armor visible
- **Base:** Mounted on modern black lacquered display base (not original)

## Key Authenticity Markers
- **Spirit cavity (藏腹):** Present on reverse — deliberate hollow used to place consecrating sacred objects (sutras, relics, grain). Now open and empty. This is a strong authenticity marker, standard practice in Chinese religious sculpture.
- **Polychrome layering:** Red, gold, and white/gesso layers with significant wear — consistent with genuine age
- **Paint loss pattern:** Exposing white gesso ground layer in a naturalistic crackle/flake pattern consistent with centuries of aging

## Condition
- Significant paint loss throughout
- Underlying wood appears stable
- Spirit cavity open, contents long gone
- Minor structural losses at extremities

## Probable Origin & Date
- **Southern Chinese workshop** — likely Fujian, Guangdong, or possibly Yunnan province
- **Ming dynasty, approximately 16th–17th century**
- Wood with gesso and polychrome lacquer — typical of southern Chinese temple sculpture

## Relationship to Collection
Almost certainly paired with Statue 2 (Red-Face Warrior) as a matched flanking set. Both share: identical scale/proportions, same polychrome palette, same spirit cavity placement and construction, same kneeling warrior posture, same headdress type. Likely flanked Statue 4 (Seated Civil Deity) as altar guardians.
