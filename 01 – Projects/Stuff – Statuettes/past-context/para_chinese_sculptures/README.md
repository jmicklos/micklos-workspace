# PARA Project: Chinese Temple Sculpture Collection
**Status:** Active  
**Owner:** Jonathan  
**Created:** June 2026  
**Goal:** Formal appraisal and provenance research for a collection of five Ming-dynasty Chinese polychrome wooden temple sculptures, with a view to auction and/or insurance valuation.

---

## Quick Context for New Sessions

This project documents a collection of five Chinese temple sculptures owned by Jonathan, located in Seattle (Capitol Hill). All five pieces have been photographed and analyzed in detail. The analysis below was produced by Claude Sonnet based on visual inspection of 6–8 photos per piece.

**Do not clean, consolidate, or restore any piece before professional appraisal.**

---

## Collection Overview

| # | Working Title | Identity | Posture | Spirit Cavity | Est. Date |
|---|--------------|----------|---------|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | Pale Warrior | Kneeling military attendant (Guan Yu retinue or Heavenly King attendant) | Kneeling, holding tablet | Yes | Ming, 16th–17th c. |
| 2 | Red-Face Warrior | Guan Yu (关羽) type or senior martial deity | Kneeling, hand damaged | Yes | Ming, 16th–17th c. |
| 3 | Zhong Kui | Zhong Kui (钟馗), Vanquisher of Demons | Seated on subdued demon (gui) | No (different construction) | Ming, 16th–17th c. |
| 4 | Seated Civil Deity | Wenchang Dijun (文昌帝君) or Taisui / Chenghuang | Seated on throne block | Yes | Ming, possibly 15th–16th c. (earliest) |
| 5 | Standing Attendant | Pan Guan / civil recording official / Lu Xing | Standing, holding register | Yes | Ming, 16th–17th c. |

**Hypothesis:** Statues 1, 2, 4, and 5 form a coherent altar ensemble (primary deity + warrior flankers + civil attendant). Statue 3 (Zhong Kui) may be from a separate altar or context within the same temple.
