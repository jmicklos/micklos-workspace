# Chinese Temple Sculpture — Iconography Reference

A reference for identifying and contextualizing the figures in this collection.

---

## Key Deity Identifications

### Guan Yu (关羽) — God of War, Brotherhood, Righteousness
- **Face:** Always red/vermilion
- **Attributes:** Green dragon crescent blade (青龙偃月刀), sometimes a book
- **Dress:** Military armor, sometimes civil robes
- **Expression:** Fierce, commanding
- **Context:** Widely worshipped in southern China (Fujian, Guangdong); appears in both Buddhist and Daoist temples
- **Relevance:** Statue 2 in this collection

### Wenchang Dijun (文昌帝君) — God of Literature and Examinations
- **Face:** Benevolent, often bearded
- **Attributes:** Ruyi scepter, scroll, writing brush, official's tablet
- **Dress:** Civil official robes, tall formal crown
- **Expression:** Calm, scholarly, dignified
- **Context:** Major deity in Ming-dynasty China; especially prominent in regions with strong examination culture
- **Relevance:** Primary candidate for Statue 4

### Zhong Kui (钟馗) — Vanquisher of Demons
- **Face:** Dark/black, fierce
- **Attributes:** Sword (often), judge's brush, bat; raised fist; subdued demon beneath
- **Dress:** Civil/scholar robes (he was a failed scholar)
- **Expression:** Fierce, triumphant
- **Context:** Folk religion; domestic and temple use; Dragon Boat Festival association
- **Relevance:** Statue 3 in this collection — definitive identification

### Pan Guan (判官) — Divine Judge/Registrar
- **Face:** Variable; often stern
- **Attributes:** Register/account book, writing brush, official tablet
- **Dress:** Civil official robes
- **Expression:** Serious, official
- **Context:** Underworld bureaucracy; attendant to major deities
- **Relevance:** Candidate for Statue 5

### Lu Xing (禄星) — Star God of Prosperity and Rank
- **Face:** Often jolly, bearded
- **Attributes:** Scroll or tablet, sometimes a child or deer
- **Dress:** Civil official robes
- **Expression:** Benevolent, slightly smiling
- **Context:** Popular deity of good fortune and official advancement
- **Relevance:** Alternative candidate for Statue 5

## Iconographic Conventions

### Face Colors
- **Pale/natural:** Neutral rank; calm guardian; civil deity
- **Red/vermilion:** Guan Yu; fierce loyalty; martial righteousness
- **Black/dark:** Zhong Kui; also some underworld deities
- **Gold:** Highest divine rank; Buddhas and supreme Daoist deities

### Posture and Hierarchy
- **Seated on throne:** Primary deity (highest rank)
- **Standing:** Attendant or secondary deity
- **Kneeling:** Subordinate attendant, guardian
- **Seated on animal/demon:** Specific iconographic identity (e.g., Zhong Kui on gui)

### Attribute Objects
- **Tablet (笏):** Civil official rank; reporting posture
- **Weapon (sword, halberd, staff):** Martial deity
- **Register/scroll:** Recording official; divine scribe
- **Ruyi scepter (如意):** High-ranking deity; wish-fulfilling power
- **Ball/pearl:** Various senior deities

## Construction Conventions

### Spirit Cavity (藏腹 zàng fù)
A deliberate hollow cut into the back of a consecrated figure, used to contain sacred objects: sutras, grain, coins, relics, or other consecrating materials. Standard practice in Chinese religious sculpture, particularly southern Chinese temple figures. An open, empty cavity indicates the figure was once active in temple worship. A strong authenticity marker — very difficult to fake convincingly in combination with appropriate aging of surrounding surfaces.

### Multi-Piece Assembly
Large temple figures were typically assembled from multiple carved sections:
- Head carved separately and pegged to body
- Arms carved separately and tenoned into shoulders
- Lower body/throne often a separate block
This construction is visible in Statues 4 and 5 of this collection.

### Gesso and Lacquer Layers
Southern Chinese temple sculpture typically used:
1. Raw wood substrate
2. Cloth/hemp fabric adhesive layer (sometimes)
3. White gesso (chalk/kaolin) ground layer
4. Colored lacquer layers (red, black, green, blue)
5. Gold leaf or gold paint highlights
6. Final varnish or lacquer seal

Aging produces characteristic crackle and flake patterns in the gesso/lacquer layers, exposing lower layers. The pattern of this exposure is a key authentication indicator.

## Regional Workshop Traditions

### Fujian Province
- Most prolific producer of southern Chinese temple sculpture
- Strong Guan Yu worship tradition
- Characteristic: rich polychrome, elaborate surface decoration, dense iconographic programs
- Export tradition to Southeast Asia (Singapore, Malaysia, Vietnam) via maritime trade

### Guangdong Province
- Similar tradition to Fujian; slightly different aesthetic
- Strong local deity (Chenghuang, Tudi Gong) worship tradition

### Jiangnan (Yangtze Delta)
- More refined, slightly more restrained aesthetic
- Strong Wenchang and civil deity worship tradition
- Zhong Kui figures frequently produced here

### Yunnan Province
- More remote tradition; some distinct stylistic features
- Less common in international market
