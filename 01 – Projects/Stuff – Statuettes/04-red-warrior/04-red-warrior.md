# 04 — Red-Face Kneeling Warrior

## What We See
- Kneeling armored figure with a deep red (vermilion) face
- Wide-set eyes, open mouth, fierce/protective expression
- Tall official's military cap
- Dragon motif on breastplate, armored sleeves, knee guards
- Left hand holds a cylindrical object (largely lost/damaged — probably a weapon or scepter originally)
- Stockier and more compact build than piece 05
- Mounted on a modern black lacquered display base (not original)
- Height: 12"

## Material
Feels like plaster or clay — not obviously wood like pieces 01-03. Could be a clay/stucco construction, or wood with an unusually thick gesso/lacquer layer. Needs expert confirmation.

## Spirit Cavity
Yes — on reverse, somewhat larger and more roughly finished than piece 05's cavity.

## Possible Inscription
The back of the lower body/belt area shows what appear to be painted marks — potentially an inscription or decorative motif. **Needs close examination by someone who can read classical Chinese.** Do not clean this area.

## AI-Guessed Identity (Unconfirmed)
**Guan Yu (关羽) type** — God of War, Brotherhood, and Righteousness. Or possibly one of the Four Heavenly Kings' generals.

Why this guess:
- Red/vermilion face is near-definitive for Guan Yu in Chinese temple sculpture
- Guan Yu was among the most widely worshipped deities in Ming-dynasty southern China
- He appears in both Buddhist and Daoist temple contexts

## AI-Guessed Date and Origin (Unconfirmed)
- Ming dynasty, 16th-17th century
- Southern Chinese workshop, likely Fujian or Guangdong (strong Guan Yu worship traditions)
- Same commission as piece 05

## Matched Pair with Piece 05
These two are almost certainly a set. They share:
- Same scale and proportions
- Same kneeling warrior posture
- Same headdress type
- Same spirit cavity placement and construction
- Same style of armor with dragon motifs
- Contrasting faces: red (fierce) vs. pale (calm) — a common southern Chinese temple convention

Together they would have flanked a central deity (piece 01) as altar guardians. Paired figures are significantly more valuable than individual pieces.

## Condition
- More paint loss on reverse than piece 05
- One hand largely missing/damaged
- Front surfaces retain more original color than reverse
- Spirit cavity open

## Keep/Sell Considerations
A kneeling warrior attendant from a temple altar — overtly religious in original context. The red face and fierce expression make it more visually intense than some of the other pieces. If selling, should be sold together with piece 05 as a pair.
