# 02 — Standing Figure with Document

## What We See
- Standing male figure — the only fully standing piece in the collection
- Both hands raised, holding a rectangular object (document, register, or tablet) in a formal presenting gesture
- Civil robes with crossed collar, belt, layered sleeves — no armor
- Short flat cap (futou style) — associated with civil officials of middle rank
- Significant insect/worm damage visible on top of the cap
- Subtle beard, warm expression with a slightly knowing quality — the best facial carving in the collection
- Multi-sided (octagonal) stepped base
- Height: 20.5"

## Material
Wood. Best overall paint preservation of the group — warm reddish-brown ground with gold highlights still visible on face, neck, and robe edges.

## Spirit Cavity
Yes — large, cleanly cut rectangular hollow on the reverse, well-preserved. Same consecration tradition as pieces 01, 04, and 05.

## AI-Guessed Identity (Unconfirmed)
**Pan Guan (判官)** — a divine judge or registrar in Chinese religious bureaucracy. Or possibly **Lu Xing (禄星)** — Star God of Prosperity and Official Rank.

Why this guess:
- Standing posture = attendant role (primary deities sit)
- Holding a document in a presenting gesture = recording official or scribe
- Civil robes + document = administrative function, not military

## AI-Guessed Date and Origin (Unconfirmed)
- Ming dynasty, 16th-17th century
- Fujian or Guangdong provincial tradition
- Facial carving quality suggests a higher-tier workshop, not a rural village commission

## Role in Ensemble
If the altar ensemble hypothesis is correct, this figure was an **attendant to the seated deity (01)** — positioned to one side, presenting documents or offerings. The standing posture and document-holding gesture make it the most "interactive" element of the group.

## Condition
- Best paint preservation of the five pieces
- Significant insect damage to cap/crown (also a strong authenticity indicator — centuries of exposure, very hard to fake)
- Some losses at robe hem/base junction
- Structurally sound

## Keep/Sell Considerations
A recording official or scribe is less overtly "religious" than a deity — more of a bureaucratic figure. The face is genuinely beautiful and has real artistic character. Possibly the most displayable piece from a "this is art, not an altar" perspective. But still part of a religious context originally. Needs expert ID.
