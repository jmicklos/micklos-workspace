# Stuff -- Statuettes

Five Chinese carved figures acquired from a gallery in Thailand (Feb 2023). All appear to be temple or shrine figures, painted and decorated with multiple colors, gilt, and lacquer. Some are clearly wood; others feel like plaster or a different material — unclear without expert confirmation.

**Primary goal: identification.** Figure out what these actually are so we can make informed keep/sell decisions. Value is secondary.

**The keep/sell calculus:** Wan Ting (Singaporean Chinese) is uneasy about them. Concerns: (1) religious objects displayed as decor feels wrong, (2) "look at this random place in Asia I mini-colonized" vibes. Pieces we keep need a story beyond "cool thing bought in Thailand." Overtly devotional temple objects are harder to justify keeping. Pieces with cultural significance, or where we can engage with them respectfully and knowledgeably, are more keepable.

**Do not clean, consolidate, or restore any piece before professional appraisal.**

## Ensemble Hypothesis (Unconfirmed)

A prior AI analysis suggested four of the five (02, 03, 04, 05) formed a coherent altar set:
- 03 = primary seated deity (center)
- 04 + 05 = matched pair of warrior guardians (flanking)
- 02 = civil attendant/scribe (to one side)
- 01 (Zhong Kui) = separate context — different construction (no spirit cavity), different type

All four ensemble pieces have spirit cavities (consecration hollows cut into the back, now open and empty). Piece 01 does not. If they really are a set, the group would be worth more together.

## What We Don't Know

Everything below is an AI guess from photos, not expert opinion:
- **What they are** — deity identifications are plausible but unconfirmed (except Zhong Kui, which is near-certain based on the demon base)
- **How old they are** — past analysis guessed Ming dynasty (1400s-1600s). Could be later.
- **What 04 and 05 are made of** — pieces 01-03 are wood; 04 and 05 feel like plaster or clay. Could be wood with unusually thick gesso/lacquer, or genuinely a different material.
- **Where they're from** — guessed southern China (Fujian/Guangdong), but unconfirmed
- **What they're worth** — no professional valuation yet. Bonhams passed.
- **Possible inscription** — piece 04 may have painted marks on the reverse (noted in past analysis, not yet examined closely)
