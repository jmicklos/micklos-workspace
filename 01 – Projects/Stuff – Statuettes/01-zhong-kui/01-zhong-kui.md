## Identity — Near Certain
**Zhong Kui (钟馗) — Vanquisher of Demons.** The combination of dark face + subdued demon beneath + scholar robes + angular cap is unmistakable.

### Who is Zhong Kui?
He occupies a unique space in Chinese culture — neither purely Buddhist nor Daoist, but deeply embedded in folk tradition. The story: a brilliant scholar who died after being unfairly denied first place in the imperial examinations. He was subsequently deified as the supreme demon-queller and protector against evil spirits. Commonly displayed in homes and temples, especially during Dragon Boat Festival, to ward off malevolent forces.

He's more folk hero than object of worship — think of him as a cultural figure rather than a devotional one.

## Keep/Sell Considerations
**Probably the easiest piece to keep.** Zhong Kui is more cultural folk hero than religious deity. Singaporean Chinese families would recognize him — he's widely known across Chinese-speaking cultures. The two-figure composition (man on demon) makes it the most visually interesting and narratively rich piece. It's also a figure that's traditionally displayed in homes, not just temples. Less "I'm worshipping a foreign god" and more "this is a cool piece of Chinese folk art with a great story."

Also: Zhong Kui figures with an intact demon base are the most collectible format.
