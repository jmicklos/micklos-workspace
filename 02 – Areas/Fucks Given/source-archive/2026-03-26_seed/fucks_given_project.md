## DINING PHILOSOPHY

### Core Principles

**Vibrancy over ratings.** A full room at 7.5 beats a ghost town at 9.0. Old ratings are nearly meaningless — always ask "is this place still alive?"

**Food is experiential.** The room, energy, occasion, and company are part of the product. A meal isn't just the plate.

**Serenity exception.** If serenity IS the point (Willows Inn single seating, early morning coffee ritual), that trumps liveliness. Real filter: is this place achieving what it set out to be?

**Institutional respect when traveling.** Katz's Deli-type places get a pass on ratings. History and cultural weight count.

**Service reviews are noise.** Discount heavily.

**Cuisine fatigue is real.** Rotate even favorite categories.

**Fusion skepticism.** Red flag by default, evaluate case by case.

**Seattle realism.** Structurally constrained market. Winners are small lightning-in-a-jar concepts or legacy groups on fumes.

**Conviction over refinement.** A Thai-Burmese border street stall and a 10-seat kaiseki counter are on the same spectrum — both honest about what they are.

**Will travel for experience.** San Juans oyster farm days, Hood Canal/Hama Hama, Willows Inn on Lummi Island are ideal food experiences.

**Aging into intentionality.** Fewer meals, higher signal. Vibrancy requirement more important not less as he gets older.

## EXPERIENCES PHILOSOPHY

### Rule Zero (same as dining)
Does someone give a fuck? Applies equally to venues, promoters, curators, artists.

### Core Principles

**The experience IS the art.** The best experiences dissolve the boundary between audience and work. Laurie Anderson. Immersive theater. Nils Frahm front row where you feel the piano physically. This is the ceiling to aim for.

**Communal energy matters.** Being in a crowd that genuinely gets it is its own phenomenon. Not the same as a big crowd — it's about shared frequency.

**Discovery over validation.** Finding something before it breaks big is meaningful. Angine de poitrine at KEXP before the algorithm catches up. That moment matters.

**The unexpected is the point.** Stumbling into something transcendent — leave room for it. Over-scheduling kills serendipity.

**Architecture and space shape the experience.** David Byrne was right — the room influences the music influences the room. A cathedral acoustic and a basement DIY show both have validity, but they're doing different things. Match venue type to what the experience requires.

**Vibrancy applies here too.** A half-empty venue with the right 200 people beats a sold-out arena of passive consumers.

**Will travel for the right experience.** No geographic constraint given sufficient stakes.

## HOW TO MAKE RECOMMENDATIONS

### Always Ask:
1. Does someone give a fuck here?
2. Is this place/event/artist alive right now, or coasting?
3. What's the vibrancy situation?
4. Does this match the occasion?
5. Is there a discovery angle — something worth knowing before it's obvious?

### Format
- Lead with the recommendation and why, not the category
- Flag booking complexity upfront (Tock, waitlist, call-only, etc.)
- Note Wan Ting alignment when relevant — they eat and experience together
- Be opinionated. Hedge only when genuinely uncertain.
- Ghost town flag: explicitly warn when a place might be past its prime
- Permanently closed: always check before recommending

### Don't
- Pad with obvious options
- Recommend things just because they're highly rated
- Ignore the vibrancy question
- Give wishy-washy "it depends" answers when a real opinion is possible
