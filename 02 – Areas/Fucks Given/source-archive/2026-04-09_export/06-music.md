## Through-Line

**Technical mastery in service of feeling.**

The question that sorts everything: **Does the music have interiority?** Not just technical facility — facility in service of something felt, expressed, invested.

---

## Nuanced Notes

### Metal / Heavy
Most heavy metal (Meshuggah, hardcore) = **gym fuel** — functional listening, not pure aesthetic expression. Don't conflate with core music identity.

**Exception — Gojira:** The message and interiority elevate it above functional. "The Art of Dying" is gym fuel that also carries genuine meaning. Sits outside the gym-fuel category.

**Boris:** Defies simple categorization. "Vanilla" described as a physiological event — Wata's guitar solo at 2:19 timed perfectly for deadlifts. Not just heavy; it's an experience. Full Boris catalog spans noise, drone, doom, shoegaze, pop.

**Deftones split:**
- *White Pony* = contemplative, atmospheric, introspective
- *Around the Fur* = physical, aggressive, gym-appropriate

### Medeski Martin & Wood
Full arc matters:
- Early work: strange and dark
- Scofield collaboration (Uberjam era): its own register — jazz that moves physically
- Late arc: a children's album that works equally well for adult ears
Don't flatten into one thing.

### Live Experience
**Nils Frahm front row** = described as a religious experience. The ceiling of solo live music.

**Kasablanca at The Shop** (Seattle) = exceptional. Right room (small, industrial, physical sound) + right music + right crowd + shared experience with Wan Ting. Seeking more of this combination in Seattle.

**Seattle's structural problem:** Gets passed over for B-dates or wrong-sized rooms. The window between "playing The Shop" and "too big" is short for rising artists in this electronic/live hybrid space.
