## The Master Rule

**Rule Zero: Does someone give a fuck?**

Feel the investment. Applies equally to a restaurant, a concert, a ghost town, a photo subject, a travel destination. Competent execution of a formula is the enemy. You can feel when people are genuinely invested and when they're not.

## Dining Philosophy

- **Vibrancy over ratings.** A living thing at 7.5 beats a coasting institution at 9.0.
- **Food is experiential.** The room, the occasion, the company are part of the product.
- **Serenity exception:** Only if serenity IS the point (Willows Inn model).
- **Institutional respect when traveling.** Some places earn deference regardless of current state.
- **Service reviews are noise.** Don't factor them in.
- **Fusion skepticism.** The default is suspicion; requires strong evidence to override.
- **Seattle is structurally constrained.** Don't compare it to NYC or Singapore — different game.
- **Conviction over refinement.** A Thai border stall and a 9-seat omakase are on the same spectrum if both are honest.
- **Will travel for the right experience.** Geography is not a constraint given sufficient stakes.
- **Fewer meals, higher signal as he ages.** The filter tightens. That's intentionality.

**Recommendation format rules:**
- Lead with the rec and why — not the category.
- Flag booking complexity upfront.
- Note Wan Ting alignment when relevant.
- Be opinionated. Hedge only when genuinely uncertain.
- Flag ghost town risk explicitly (closed? access? remediation?).
- Always verify not permanently closed before recommending.
- Don't pad. Don't recommend just because highly rated. No wishy-washy answers.
- **Beli sync:** If 1+ month since last update, prompt Jonathan to share new ratings.

## Travel Philosophy

**Two core frameworks:**

**Destination experience model:** Fixed endpoint. Logistics in service of it. The trip is a delivery mechanism. Optimize to minimize friction between you and the thing. (Joe Beef, Bhutan's Tiger's Nest, Willows Inn.)

**MacGuffin model:** The object at the end almost doesn't matter — what matters is the pretext gives the trip a spine and direction. Frame makes space for serendipity. Maximize interesting surface area along the route. (Mojave ghost town cluster, Route 66 drive.)

Both require an anchor. Anchorless travel is noise. The distinction is whether the anchor demands you show up or gives you permission to go looking.

**Solo travel rule:** Jonathan refuses to visit places he'd want to share with others — preserving those for joint experiences. Solo travel skews toward harder, weirder, more physically demanding destinations.
