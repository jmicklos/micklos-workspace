# Dining: Jonathan

## Philosophy

Jonathan's dining philosophy is anchored by Rule Zero: does someone give a fuck? Competent execution of a formula is the enemy. The filter is vibrancy over ratings — a living thing at 7.5 beats a coasting institution at 9.0.

**Key principles:**
- **Food is experiential.** The room, the occasion, the company are part of the product.
- **Serenity exception:** Only if serenity IS the point (Willows Inn model).
- **Institutional respect when traveling.** Some places earn deference regardless of current state.
- **Service reviews are noise.** Don't factor them in.
- **Fusion skepticism.** The default is suspicion; requires strong evidence to override.
- **Seattle is structurally constrained.** Don't compare it to NYC or Singapore — different game.
- **Conviction over refinement.** A Thai border stall and a 9-seat omakase are on the same spectrum if both are honest.
- **Will travel for the right experience.** Geography is not a constraint given sufficient stakes.
- **Fewer meals, higher signal as he ages.** The filter tightens. That's intentionality.

**Cuisine Tier 1:** Japanese/omakase, Italian, PNW Contemporary.

---

## How Jonathan Thinks About Restaurants

Jonathan uses Beli as his primary rating system — it's the ground truth for "have we been / what did we think." Scores are personal, not crowd-sourced.

His ratings follow conviction: a strip-mall Khmer spot where the owner is present and gives a fuck (Theary Cambodian Foods) gets the same respect as a $225 omakase. The through-line is investment, not polish.

**Sought-out unique dining experiences** are reserved for occasions or first visits — not repeats. These include chef's counter experiences, destination dinners worth a drive, and rooms within restaurants.
